# 3D

BUILD-PLAN.md §8. Library: Three.js via React Three Fiber and Drei.

## Status: not in Phase 0–3

3D is deferred until the customer ordering slice works end to end. §8 says it
plainly — "3D is an enhancement, not the foundation" — and §140 requires it to
be disposable.

There is a second reason, and it is the more important one. The site's real gap
is that **there is no food photography.** A fried chicken brand is carried by
pictures of the chicken. A rotating 3D object is a workaround for the missing
photos, and shipping the workaround makes the gap permanent.

The order is: real photos, then judge whether 3D still adds anything.

## If and when it ships

**Allowed:** the hero on desktop, a product or brand visual, an interactive
campaign section, a seasonal experience.

**Never:** the menu, the cart, checkout, the POS, the KDS, data tables, or a
card component.

## Non-negotiables

- Dynamically imported. Three.js never enters the bundle for a page that does
  not render it, and never for the customer's critical path (§49).
- Loads after critical content. It never blocks LCP.
- A static poster image is the fallback, and it must look finished on its own —
  not a grey box.
- Works with WebGL unavailable, on a weak device, and under reduced motion.
- **Never blocks checkout.** If the renderer fails, ordering continues.

## Assets

GLB/GLTF, compressed geometry (DRACO or Meshopt), compressed textures, lazy
loaded. Budget: under 1.5MB for the hero object, or it does not ship.

## The test

Turn WebGL off. If the page still sells the food and takes the order, the 3D
was an enhancement. If it does not, the 3D was load-bearing and needs removing.
